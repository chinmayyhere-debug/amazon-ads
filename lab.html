Amazon Ads measurement lab · Chinmay Parmar

Which Amazon ads actually sold more coffee?

Northfield Coffee Co. sells on Amazon.ca and spends about $140K a week across Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Prime Video ads and Amazon DSP video. Their marketing lead asks three questions. This lab answers them with SQL and Python, and lets you test every answer yourself.

The question
Did our holiday campaign cause extra sales?
And which ad product deserves the credit and the budget?
The method
Holdout test, attribution, segments, MMM
40,000 shoppers, 218,000 ad impressions, 3 years of weekly spend.
The answer
Yes, +30% lift. Shift $19K/week to Sponsored Products.
Same budget, +4.6% incremental sales.

02 · Media attribution

Attribution rewards reach, not impact

Each model splits credit for a purchase across the ads a shopper saw. The black tick shows each product's true share of the extra purchases, recorded by the simulator.

In plain words

The ad seen most often is not always the ad that changed the decision. Credit models count views; the lift test measures impact.

Model

First and last touch use SQL ROW_NUMBER() windows over each journey. Markov credits a product by how many purchases disappear when it is removed from the paths.

Model creditTrue share of extra purchases
TakeawayEvery rule-based model gives broad-reach DSP video about 3.6x the credit it earned, and gives Sponsored Products 22 points too little. Budget decisions based on these models would move money the wrong way.
03 · Customer segmentation

Who actually responds to ads

K-Means groups shoppers by recency, frequency, spend, tenure and Prime status, using only the year before the campaign. Then the holdout lift is measured inside each group. Select a segment.

In plain words

Loyal coffee buyers buy more when reminded. Shoppers who stopped buying a year ago ignore the ads, so those impressions are wasted.

04 · Marketing mix model

Plan next quarter's budget

An MMM fit on 156 weeks of spend and sales. It models carryover (an ad keeps working after the week it ran) and saturation (each extra dollar does less), with trend, seasonality, Q4 and promotions controlled for.

In plain words

Drag the sliders to set weekly spend. The model predicts the extra sales. Where a curve is flattening, money is better spent elsewhere.

Solid dot: your spend. Dashed line: today's spend. "Optimize" keeps your total and gives each next $500 to the product with the best return.

What went wrong while building it

  
My first MMM said Prime Video ads returned 10x. The truth was 2.85x. I had forced every coefficient to be positive, including seasonality terms that need to go negative, so the ads absorbed normal baseline sales. Constraining only the media coefficients fixed it, taking the fit from R² 0.83 to 0.975. A grid search over carryover settings also got stuck; a global optimizer did not.
